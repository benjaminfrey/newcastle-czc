# Summary of Changes — v1.0

**For adoption at Town Meeting, September 14, 2026.** Changes are stated against the
Core Zoning Code adopted November 3, 2020 and amended through March 24, 2025.

This summary exists because a redline cannot show everything. Three things in particular are
invisible in the marked-up document, and each is explained below: the new Article 3 (marked
entirely as an addition, which tells a reader nothing about what matters in it), the renumbering
of every article after Article 2, and the maps, tables and exhibits (which are printed as they
now stand, unmarked).

---

## What is new

### Article 3, Thoroughfares

The Town's Road, Driveway and Entrance Ordinance is repealed, and its subject matter is brought
into the Zoning Code as a new Article 3. Because the article is entirely new, the redline marks
all of it as added. That mark says nothing about which parts matter, so the substance is set out
here.

**Ten Thoroughfare Types.** Every street, road and lane in Newcastle is assigned one Type, which
sets its width, right-of-way, design speed, surface, and whether it has sidewalks or planting
strips. The Types are:

| Streets (village and neighborhood) | Roads (rural and highway) |
| :--- | :--- |
| S1 Main Street | R1 Connector Road |
| S2 Village Street | R2 Rural Road |
| S3 Neighborhood Street | R3 Rural Lane |
| S4 Lane | R4 Highway Commercial |
| S5 Alley | R5 Rural Highway |

**Ownership is kept separate from Type.** A road's Type describes its physical form. Who owns and
maintains it is a separate question, answered by one of four Ownership Categories: Town Way,
Public Easement, Private Road, and State Highway. Changing a road's Type does not change who owns
it, who maintains it, or who may use it.

**Every road in town is listed.** Section 5 classifies existing thoroughfares, and the results are
printed as Exhibit 3.1 (the Inventory) and Exhibit 3.2 (the Type Map): **214 segments across 148
named roads.** By Type:

| Type | Segments | | Type | Segments |
| :--- | ---: | :--- | :--- | ---: |
| R2 Rural Road | 70 | | S1 Main Street | 14 |
| R3 Rural Lane | 66 | | R5 Rural Highway | 8 |
| R1 Connector Road | 25 | | R4 Highway Commercial | 6 |
| S3 Neighborhood Street | 18 | | S2 Village Street | 5 |
| | | | S4 Lane | 2 |

No segment in Newcastle is an S5 Alley today, so that Type appears in the standards but not on the
map. By ownership: 105 Private Road, 79 Town Way, 28 State Highway. Two segments — Kavanagh Road
and Woods Island Road — matched no ownership record and are shown with a dash rather than a guess.

**Driveways stay driveways.** Section 7 governs driveways. A driveway remains a driveway, and no
street or road standard reaches it, unless and until it is expanded or altered to serve more than
two single-family homes or more than one two-family home. Classifying a way in the Inventory does
not by itself require anyone to improve anything. Sixty-one segments are recorded as functioning
today as driveways; that record is descriptive and is not what decides the question.

**Street trees.** Section 3.G is new. On Streets (S1–S5) it requires planting, and it protects
Significant Street Trees — a healthy tree at least 6 inches in caliper whose root zone or canopy
reaches into the right-of-way. Removing such a tree for Town or applicant work in the
right-of-way requires replacement in kind and, in some cases, review by a certified arborist.
Utilities coordinate and restore; nothing here overrides State or utility rights. Ordinary
maintenance does not include removing a healthy mature tree.

**Sidewalks on new streets.** Section 3.F sets out when a sidewalk must be provided on a new
thoroughfare: on both sides of a Street in the Village Business and Town Center districts; on at
least one side within a quarter mile of a school, the Town Office, the library, the post office,
a public park or landing, or a place of public assembly; where a segment connects to an existing
sidewalk; and on at least one side of a new Street serving more than 12 dwelling units. Rural
Road Types (R1–R5) are exempt unless the segment abuts Village Business or Town Center frontage.

### New definitions

About twenty-seven terms are added to Article 9, nearly all supporting Article 3: the ten Type
names, the four Ownership Categories, and the vocabulary the standards use — Cartway, Travel Lane,
On-Street Parking Lane, Planting Strip, Curb Return Radius, Build-To Standard, Provision Component,
Thoroughfare, Thoroughfare Type, Critical Root Zone, Significant Street Tree, and Certified
Arborist.

**Character** is now defined. The definition ties the word to physical and design attributes —
building form, scale and placement, the pattern of lots and streets, the streetscape — and states
plainly that it does **not** refer to who owns or occupies property or to the socioeconomic
composition of an area.

## What changed

### The articles were renumbered

Every article after Article 2 moves up by one to make room for Article 3: old Article 3 becomes 4,
4 becomes 5, 5 becomes 6, 6 becomes 7, 7 becomes 8, and 8 becomes 9. Table numbers and internal
cross-references were updated to match. **This renumbering is not marked in the redline** — marking
it would have added change marks to well over a hundred lines that say nothing. It is stated here
instead. Do not read the absence of renumbering marks as meaning nothing was renumbered.

### Article 2, District Standards

The prose sections of Article 2 changed in three places, all of them consequences of Article 3:

- Newly created lots must abut a **Thoroughfare of a Type defined in Article 3**, rather than "a
  Public Road or Private Road which conforms with the Newcastle Driveway, Road, and Entrance
  Ordinance."
- The 24-foot right-of-way required for a new insular lot now runs to a Thoroughfare as defined in
  Article 3.
- For a corner or through lot fronting two roads, primary frontage was previously along "the
  Public or Private Road of greatest significance" — a judgment with no stated method. It is now
  determined by the Type hierarchy: Streets take precedence over Roads, and within each family the
  lower-numbered Type takes precedence. This replaces a judgment call with a rule anyone can apply
  and get the same answer.

No district standard has been changed, added, or removed.

### Article 4, Site Standards (formerly Article 3)

- The **Driveways** section previously carried six rules of its own and required compliance with
  the Roads, Driveways and Entrances Ordinance. It is now a cross-reference: driveways are
  regulated under Article 3 Section 7, which is where all driveway design, location, sharing and
  calibration standards now live. Nothing was dropped; it moved.
- **Off-street parking access** previously had to come from "an alley or secondary road." It now
  reads "a Lane or Alley (Types S4 or S5)" or a secondary frontage — the same rule, stated in the
  Type vocabulary.

### Article 8, Administration (formerly Article 7)

- The subdivision review standards previously required conformance with "The Newcastle Road,
  Driveway, and Entrance Ordinance." That now reads "The standards of Article 3 Thoroughfares."
- **Master Plan approval now fixes thoroughfare design.** At Master Plan approval, the Type and
  cross-section configuration of each thoroughfare in the plan are fixed and govern later phases.
  A later phase may propose a different Type or cross-section only by revising the Master Plan.
  This is a new constraint; previously nothing fixed those choices between phases.
- Two review criteria that turned on the "character" of a proposal now read "nature" instead, so
  that the newly defined term is not doing work in a standard where it was never intended to.

### Article 9, Definitions (formerly Article 8)

Beyond the new terms, four existing definitions were rewritten:

- **Alley** was a type of road; it is now the S5 Type. Alley and Lane were previously one combined
  type; they are now distinct — a Lane (S4) has buildings fronting on it, an Alley (S5) serves the
  side or rear.
- **Driveway** was defined by reference to construction specifications for roads. It is now defined
  as a private access way from a Street or Road to the interior of a single lot, with Article 3
  Section 7 governing. Any earlier use-based definition is superseded.
- **Road, Private** and **Road, Public** previously read, in their entirety, "See Newcastle Road
  Ordinance." That ordinance is repealed, so both now carry real definitions. "Road, Public" is
  identified as a legacy term and construed to include both Town Way and Public Easement.

### Two errors in the adopted Code are corrected

- The definitions in the adopted Code point readers to "Article 4 Use Standards" in eight places.
  Use Standards was Article 6, not Article 4. All eight now point to Article 7, where Use Standards
  now sits.
- The adopted Code numbers **two different tables "Table 3.1"** — the Screening Formula and Site
  Lumens. They are now Table 4.1 and Table 4.2 respectively.

### The maps, tables and exhibits are printed as they now stand

Figures, tables and maps cannot be marked by a text comparison, so they appear in the redline at
their current state with no marks. That includes the ten Type pages in Article 3 Section 2,
Exhibit 3.1 and Exhibit 3.2, and the district spreads. Where those changed, the change is
described in words above.

## What did not change

**Article 1 General, Article 5 Building Standards, Article 6 Design Standards, and Article 7 Use
Standards contain no substantive change at all** — only the renumbering of cross-references
described above. Every use permitted or prohibited in each district, every building standard, and
every design standard is exactly as adopted.

Nothing in this Code requires any existing road, driveway or building to be changed. The Inventory
records what exists; it does not require anyone to improve anything.

---

## Was anything made stricter than the adopted Code?

**Yes, in the following places.** Stating them plainly is more useful than a general reassurance:

1. **Street tree protection (Article 3 Section 3.G) is a new obligation.** Nothing in the adopted
   Code protected an existing street tree. Removing a Significant Street Tree for Town or applicant
   work in a Street right-of-way now requires in-kind replacement and may require arborist review.
2. **A driveway that grows must be improved (Article 3 Section 7.F).** A driveway expanded to serve
   three or four dwellings must meet a safety-critical core standard; at five or more, or on
   subdivision, or if accepted as a Town Way, it must meet its full Type. The adopted Zoning Code
   set no such threshold — this subject sat in the Road, Driveway and Entrance Ordinance, which is
   repealed. This applies only when a driveway is expanded or altered, never to a driveway left
   as it is.
3. **Master Plan phasing (Article 8).** Thoroughfare Type and cross-section are fixed at Master
   Plan approval and can only be changed by revising the Master Plan.

Against that, some standards are **lighter** than what a road would otherwise have had to meet.
The most consequential is the R3 Rural Lane Type, which applies to 66 segments: a 12-foot gravel
way in a 33-foot right-of-way, against the R2 Rural Road standard of an 18-foot two-lane way in a
40-foot right-of-way. The safety standards — sight distance, maximum grade, drainage — are
identical between the two. Where a small lane is classified R3 rather than R2, less would be
required of it if it were ever improved, not more.

No dimension, setback, height, use permission or district standard elsewhere in the Code was made
stricter.
