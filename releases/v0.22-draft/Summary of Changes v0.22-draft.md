# Summary of Changes — v0.22-draft

Article 3 (Thoroughfares): two changes in response to resident comments — clearer rules about
when a driveway must be improved to a road or street, and new protection for existing street
trees. Builds on v0.21-draft.

## Driveways listed in the inventory ("road stubs")

Residents noted that many short "road stubs" shown on the Type map and listed in the inventory
are, today, driveways. This release makes the Code's intent explicit and adds reassurance:

- **Adopting this Article does not require anyone to upgrade an existing driveway or road.**
  Existing ways may continue as they are. The Type assigned to a segment becomes the required
  standard only when the way is newly built, substantially reconstructed, or grows past the
  driveway limit.
- **When a driveway grows past the driveway limit** — more than two single-family homes, or more
  than one two-family home — **it is no longer a driveway** and must be improved to the road or
  street Type already assigned to it in the inventory.
- **The required improvement is right-sized to the number of homes:**
  - **Three or four homes:** only the safety essentials of the assigned Type are required — the
    travel-way width, drainage and culverts, sight lines, intersection geometry, and an
    emergency-vehicle turnaround. Sidewalks, planting strips, street trees, and on-street parking
    are not required, and the full right-of-way need not be dedicated, as long as the safety items
    fit the existing way.
  - **Five or more homes, a subdivision, or a road offered to the Town:** the way must be built to
    the full standard of its assigned Type.
  - A safety floor applies regardless of the number of homes: any stream or Shoreland crossing
    must be engineered, and the reviewing authority may require more where site conditions warrant.
- **No new determination or Town Meeting vote is needed when this happens.** Because every
  segment's Type is already set in the adopted inventory, the required standard is known in
  advance — there is no case-by-case Type determination by the Code Enforcement Officer or
  Planning Board, and no ordinance amendment, at the time a property is developed. (A segment's
  Type changes only through the inventory's reclassification process.)
- **The inventory's source is noted.** A line on the inventory table now explains that segment
  names and locations come from the Town's E-911 road data and that the recorded widths and other
  field values are approximate.

## Street trees

In response to a comment about protecting mature street trees when sidewalk, maintenance, or
utility work occurs, a new "Street Trees" subsection was added. It applies to Streets (the urban
"S" Types), not to rural Roads:

- **Planting** of street trees on Streets is required where a sidewalk is required, as before,
  with a modest strengthening — trees are also called for where a planting strip is built without
  a required sidewalk, and the Code Enforcement Officer may approve street trees elsewhere along a
  Street where space and growing conditions allow.
- **Existing significant street trees are protected.** A "significant street tree" is a healthy
  existing street tree at least six inches in caliper. When the Town, or an applicant, performs
  work in a Street's right-of-way:
  - the work must avoid disturbing the tree's critical root zone unless there is no feasible
    alternative;
  - if a significant street tree must be removed, it must be replaced in kind on the same segment;
    and
  - work near such a tree is reviewed by a certified arborist.
- **Utilities** are asked to give notice, coordinate to avoid root damage, and restore the
  planting strip and any tree removed — the Code recognizes that it cannot override a utility's
  rights under State law.
- **Routine maintenance** of a Street does not include removing a healthy significant street tree.
- The Planning Board, when approving a new Street, is now directed to consider the establishment
  and continuity of street-tree canopy — paralleling the existing direction to weigh rural
  character on rural Roads.
- Three new definitions support these provisions: **Critical Root Zone**, **Significant Street
  Tree**, and **Certified Arborist**.
