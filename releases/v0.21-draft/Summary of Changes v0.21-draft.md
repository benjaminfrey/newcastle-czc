# Summary of Changes — v0.21-draft

Article 3 (Thoroughfares): classification corrections, a recalibrated Main Street
cross-section, a numbered inventory, and a new rule that scales required engineering to the
road type. Builds on v0.20-draft.

## Thoroughfare classification (§5 Inventory)

- **Academy Hill** is now **Neighborhood Street (S3)** end to end; two of its segments had
  been mis-typed (as a Rural Road and as a Highway Commercial road).
- The end of **Stonebridge Circle** is now **Rural Road (R2)**, matching Timber Lane (it had
  been mis-typed as a Neighborhood Street).
- The **Route 1** segment between Lewis Hill / Lynch Road and Woods Island Road is now
  **Rural Highway (R5)**, matching the rest of the corridor.

## Inventory table (Exhibit 3.1)

- Every row is now **numbered (1–215)**, for easy reference.

## Main Street (S1)

- The cross-section is narrowed to a downtown-realistic width: **sidewalks 6 ft**, **planting
  strips 2–6 ft** (3 ft typical), and the **right-of-way recomputed to 50–62 ft** (about 56 ft
  typical). Travel lanes and on-street parking are unchanged; the cross-section drawing is
  updated to match.
- A note was added to the Main Street page: like the Highway Commercial (R4) and Rural Highway
  (R5) Types, **Main Street follows a State-owned route — U.S. Route 1B — and MaineDOT has
  jurisdiction over the traveled way within its right-of-way.** Main Street keeps its full Town
  design standards.

## New thoroughfares (§6): engineering scaled to the road type

New streets and roads now require a level of engineering and oversight that matches their
Type, instead of one standard for all (new Table 3.5):

- **Basic** — Lane (S4), Alley (S5), Rural Lane (R3): an applicant-prepared plan, no
  engineer's stamp; Town review and inspection.
- **Engineered** — Village Street (S2), Neighborhood Street (S3), Connector Road (R1), Rural
  Road (R2): engineer-stamped plans, engineer-supervised construction, and inspection.
- **State** — Main Street (S1), Highway Commercial (R4), Rural Highway (R5): MaineDOT
  engineers and oversees the roadway; the Town reviews the streetscape.

Regardless of Type, the Town still reviews and inspects **every** new thoroughfare; any
**stream or Shoreland crossing** must be engineered; a road offered to the Town as a public
way must meet the Town's acceptance standards; and the reviewing authority may require more
engineering where site conditions warrant. (Adding this rule shifts the later §6 subsections —
Design Standards, Construction, and Acceptance — down one letter.)

## Emergency-vehicle turnarounds (§6)

- A **dead-end thoroughfare longer than 150 ft must provide a turnaround** — a hammerhead,
  cul-de-sac, or approved equivalent — sized for the largest emergency vehicle serving the
  Town. This restores a standard from the former Road, Driveway & Entrance Ordinance.

## Definitions (Article 9)

- The thoroughfare-related definitions added for Article 3 (Cartway, Thoroughfare, Street, the
  Type names, etc.) are now **merged alphabetically** into the main definitions list — they had
  been grouped in a separate section at the end. The **Alley** and **Driveway** entries were
  updated to their current Article-3 forms.

## Layout

- Removed **two redundant blank pages** that had been appearing before the District Standards
  spreads (the integrated draft is now 116 pages with a single front-matter blank).

## Not changed

- **"Cartway"** was reviewed but kept: it is already a defined term in the Code (the full
  improved surface — travel lanes, parking, and shoulders), distinct from the traveled way.
