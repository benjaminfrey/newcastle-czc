# Summary of Changes — v0.24-draft

Article 3 (Thoroughfares): the Inventory now records which listed ways are **driveways today**, 56
small private ways are reclassified to the lighter **Rural Lane (R3)** standard, and the two
exhibits are easier to read. No standard, definition, or rule text changed in this release.
Builds on v0.23-draft.

## Fifty-seven more ways recorded as driveways

v0.23 added a "present use" record to the Inventory so a segment that functions today as a driveway
is shown as **D**, with the Type it *would* take if it were ever expanded shown separately under
"On conversion." That release marked four. This one marks **fifty-seven more, across 57 roads** —
including Bell Cove Road, Brick Hill Road, Cole Cabin Road, Cottril Road, East View Road, Falls
Road, Fern Birch Lane, Forest Road, Goose Cove Lane, Gray Road, and many other short private ways.

**Sixty-one segments are now shown as driveways.** Fifty-nine are privately owned; two are Town
Ways (Hillside Road and Robinson Road).

Nothing about this requires anyone to do anything. As stated at the top of the Inventory table and
in Section 7, a driveway remains a driveway — no street or road standard reaches it — unless and
until it is expanded to serve more than two single-family homes, or more than one two-family home.
Recording present use is descriptive; it is not what decides whether something is a driveway.

## Fifty-six small ways moved to the lighter Rural Lane standard

Fifty-three segments that had been carrying the automatic Rural Road (R2) classification, plus three
others, are now **Rural Lane (R3)**. Across the whole inventory, R2 falls from 126 segments to 70
and R3 rises from 10 to 66.

**This reduces what could ever be required of these ways, and does not increase it.** Rural Lane is
the narrower of the two rural standards — a 12 ft gravel way in a 33 ft right-of-way, against Rural
Road's 18 ft two-lane way in a 40 ft right-of-way. A small lane classified R3 rather than R2 has a
*smaller* obligation if it is ever improved.

One segment moved the other way, from R3 to R2.

## How the review was done

The Town's E-911 address data records one point for every addressed structure. Counting the
residential addresses on each segment gives a direct reading of how many homes it serves, which is
the same threshold Section 7 uses to distinguish a driveway from a road.

Two things about that method are worth stating plainly:

- It was used to **sort the work, not to decide it.** Every entry above was reviewed and set by
  hand. The address count told the reviewer where to look first.
- About a quarter of the Town's address points carry no record of what kind of structure they are.
  Twelve of the ways recorded as driveways have no residential address on file but do have a
  structure of unrecorded type. Those were judged individually rather than treated as empty.

## The two exhibits are easier to read

- **The District column has been removed from the Inventory table.** It was not useful for finding
  a road or for understanding its standard, and the space now goes to the road name and the
  from-to description, which were being cut short. The table is a page shorter. The Town still
  records adjacent districts for each segment; the table simply no longer prints them.
- **The Type Map now shows the outline of the Town.** Previously the map was a network of roads
  floating on a blank page, which made it hard to tell which part of Newcastle you were looking at.
  The outline is drawn lightly, beneath the roads, purely so a reader can place a road in the town.
  It sets no boundary and establishes nothing.

## What did not change

No standard, dimension, or requirement was made stricter anywhere in this release. Nothing in it
requires any existing road or driveway to be improved. The ten Thoroughfare Types and their
standards are unchanged, as is every rule in Article 3.
