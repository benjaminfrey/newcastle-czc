## Part 1 — Reconciled definitions (proposed Article 9 set)

The principle: **keep our architecture** (Thoroughfare = umbrella; Street/Road = the S/R Type families), **borrow the other planner's cleaner "right-of-way = easement" framing**, and **fix our own "Road" definition** (still the obsolete physical-route text). One coherent set replaces both drafts' conflicting terms.

### Right-of-Way *(adopt their framing)*
> A type of easement, with defined boundaries, that allows passage and/or access by a specified party — which may include the general public — across or along an area of land. A right-of-way may or may not be improved.

**Right-of-Way, Public:** A right-of-way in which the general public holds established rights of access or passage.
**Right-of-Way, Private:** A right-of-way in which the general public holds no established rights of access or passage.

*Changed:* replaces our "total width of land reserved as a road, alley…" with the easement framing (cleaner, and it nests Public/Private). **Dependency — see caveat in Part 4:** "right-of-way **width**" is still meaningful (a bounded easement has a width), but every existing "right-of-way" usage in Articles 2–3 should be read once for consistency before this is adopted.

### Thoroughfare *(keep ours; reconcile with ROW + Driveway)*
> The umbrella term for any Street, Road, or Driveway. A Street or Road occupies a Right-of-Way and is classified as one of the ten Thoroughfare Types (S1–S5, R1–R5) under Article 3 Section 2; a Driveway provides private lot access, need not lie within a Right-of-Way, and is not assigned a Type.

*Changed:* keeps our umbrella (incl. Driveway), but states the Street/Road-occupy-a-ROW vs. Driveway-need-not relationship — which resolves the conflict with their ROW-based definition.

### Street *(keep ours; absorb their "in-town" sense)*
> A Thoroughfare classified within the Street family of Types (S1 Main Street, S2 Village Street, S3 Neighborhood Street, S4 Lane, S5 Alley) under Article 3 Section 2. Streets serve the Town's more urban Districts, where pedestrian comfort and building frontage are primary design considerations, and typically carry curbs, sidewalks, and street trees.

### Road *(NEW — fixes our inconsistency; parallels Street; absorbs their "rural" sense)*
> A Thoroughfare classified within the Road family of Types (R1 Connector Road, R2 Rural Road, R3 Rural Lane, R4 Highway Commercial, R5 Rural Highway) under Article 3 Section 2. Roads serve the Town's more rural Districts and its state-aid corridors, generally favoring vehicular movement, with shoulders and open ditch-and-culvert drainage rather than curbs and continuous sidewalks.

*Changed:* **retires the obsolete physical-route definition** ("a route, track, or way consisting of a bed of exposed mineral soil…") in favor of the R-family concept — exactly the fix we already made for "Street." This single edit is worth doing **regardless** of the merge (see Part 4).

### Driveway *(keep ours; absorb their "not within a ROW")*
> A vehicular way, not itself a Thoroughfare Type, providing access from a Street, Road, or Right-of-Way to the interior of a single lot — including to dwellings, parking areas, or loading areas. A Driveway is not located within a Right-of-Way and is not built to the construction standards required for Streets and Roads. Driveways are regulated under Article 3 Section 7.

### Thoroughfare Type *(unchanged — current Article 9 text)*
> The form-based classification assigned to a Thoroughfare under Article 3 Section 2, describing its character, dimensions, and design standards. Distinct from the Thoroughfare's Ownership Category.

### Draft text for the HIGH-priority items

**Item 1 — §10.D.2.b (culvert embedment).** Replace *"placed at stream bed level over a prepared base"* with:
> b. A culvert shall be placed, over a prepared base, so that between one-quarter and one-third of its diameter is below the natural stream bed, in order to retain natural substrate and enable unobstructed fish passage in either direction.

**Item 2 — §6 New Thoroughfares, Design Standards (new clause).**
> A dead-end Thoroughfare shall terminate in a turnaround — a hammerhead, cul-de-sac, or other configuration approved by the Road Commissioner — sized to accommodate the largest emergency apparatus serving the Town and located no more than **[150]** feet from the end of the traveled way. *(Bracket the distance for the Board / Fire Department.)*

**Item 3 — §6 New Thoroughfares, Design Standards (new clause).**
> a. No new Thoroughfare shall be located closer than **30 feet** to the lot line of a directly abutting property that does not hold deeded access to it.
> b. Non-intersecting Thoroughfares shall be separated by at least **50 feet**, measured between their nearest right-of-way edges.

### Notes on the MED / POLICY items
- **#4 (materials/underdrainage):** our Type standards already carry a "Surface" row (Paved / Gravel) and defer pavement to Table 3.3. Their tables make **material (gravel vs. macadam)** and **underdrainage (yes/no)** explicit *per type*. Folding these into **Table 3.3** (or a new column in the Type plates) gives builders a clearer default without changing our form-based structure.
- **#6 (volume caps):** sound engineering — it prevents overloading a low-volume rural lane. Suggested shape: *"An R3 Rural Lane or S4 Lane serving more than [N] dwelling units shall be designed to the next-higher Type."* This couples capacity to Type and dovetails with our §5.E reclassification and §6.D component rules — but **it is a policy choice** (it limits units by road type), so the Board should set [N] or decline.
- **#7 (one-way / advisory):** rather than add new Types (which would dilute the transect), allow a **one-way or advisory-shoulder *configuration*** of S3/S4 where the right-of-way is constrained — a §3 option, not a new Type.

## Part 3 — What NOT to harvest (and why)
- **Their function-based Type names** (Track / Small / Advisory / Local / Industrial) — conflict with our place-based transect naming and would undermine the form-based fit.
- **Deletion of "Main Street"** — we keep **S1 Main Street**; the Town Center / downtown standard depends on it.
- **Their narrower utilitarian ROW widths** — ours are deliberately calibrated to the form-based public realm (trees + walks both sides); don't downsize wholesale.
- **Their ROW-based "Thoroughfare" definition and driveway exclusion** — we keep the umbrella (Part 1).
- **Separating driveways into Site Standards** — we keep driveways integrated in Article 3 §7, calibrated by District.

---

## Part 4 — Suggested sequencing

1. **Do now, independent of the merge —** the **"Road" definition fix** (Part 1). It only corrects our own internal inconsistency (Road still reads as the old physical route while Article 3 treats it as the R-family). Safe, self-contained; a natural next-version edit.
2. **Fold in the HIGH-priority engineering items (1–3)** once the Board is comfortable — low-controversy adds that genuinely improve our new-road standards.
3. **Adopt "Right-of-Way = easement" + Public/Private ROW only with a consistency pass** over every "right-of-way" usage in Articles 2–3 (the dependency caveat). Best done as its own small, checked edit.
4. **Put the POLICY items (6–7) to the Board** before drafting.
5. **The full reconciliation** — one merged Thoroughfares article, one definition set, one Article-3 numbering — is the large step and **must be coordinated with the other planner and the Board before any warrant.** This worksheet is the input to that conversation.
