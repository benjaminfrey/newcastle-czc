**Status:** **Open — counsel review requested.** The flagged language is **retained verbatim** in the draft Code (v0.20-draft). This is the higher-risk of the two items flagged during the v0.20 "character" cleanup, and it concerns more than the single word.

## 1. Background

While defining "Character" and removing it from operative standards in **v0.20-draft**, I left the **Human Service Facility** special-permit standards (Art. 7 §34.b) untouched, because rewording approval criteria for a use class commonly associated with a **protected class** is a legal question, not an editorial one. The "character" phrase here is only the surface of a deeper concern.

**Current language — Art. 7 §34.b STANDARDS:**

- **§34.b.1** requires the applicant to "document the community's **need** for the proposed facility" and describe the number of individuals served, programs, hours, "degree of supervision," and impacts.
- **§34.b.2** permits approval "only where the use is found to be consistent with the following criteria: The proposed use will not alter the essential nature and **character of the community**; The proposed use will not result in a **duplication of services** … that would hinder the **community integration goals** of the facility and create **service capacity in excess of the overall need** …" — with relevant considerations including "the **distance** between the proposed facility and … other facilities which offer the same services" and "the **total capacity** of all similar human service facilities in the community."

## 2. The concern (FHA / Maine Human Rights Act)

Human service facilities frequently house or serve **persons with disabilities** (including people in recovery), a class protected by the federal **Fair Housing Act (FHA)** and the **Maine Human Rights Act**. Zoning criteria of exactly this shape have been **repeatedly challenged and struck down**:

- **"Need" / "public need" tests** applied to a protected-class use, where comparable uses face no such test → disparate treatment.
- **"Overconcentration," "duplication of services," distance/dispersal, and aggregate-capacity caps** → facially discriminatory in several jurisdictions; they single out the protected use.
- **Discretionary "character of the community" findings** → the vagueness/animus vehicle through which neighborhood opposition is channeled.
- The Code must also afford **reasonable accommodation** from such standards; §34 contains no accommodation pathway.

In short, §34.b applies a *need + overconcentration + character* gate to a protected-class use that the Code does not apply to comparable residential or institutional uses — the classic FHA fact pattern.

## 3. What was (not) done

Left **verbatim** in v0.20-draft. I did **not** narrow it editorially, because (a) the right fix may be to remove or restructure multiple criteria, not swap one word, and (b) that decision needs legal review.

## 4. Questions for counsel

1. Are the **need**, **duplication/overconcentration**, **aggregate-capacity**, **distance/dispersal**, and **"character of the community"** criteria in §34.b defensible under the FHA and the Maine Human Rights Act as applied to facilities serving persons with disabilities?
2. Should §34 be revised to (a) strike the need/overconcentration/character criteria, (b) apply **use-neutral** site standards equivalent to comparable uses, and (c) add an express **reasonable-accommodation** provision?
3. Should "Human Service Facility" be **reclassified** to track a comparable residential/institutional use rather than carry a bespoke discretionary standard?
4. Is any part of §34 derived from statute or a model ordinance that constrains how it may be revised?

**Recommendation:** treat §34 (definition **and** standards) as a single review item; the "character" wording is the least of it. Until counsel advises, the provision stands as written.

*This memo is issue-spotting by the code drafter for legal review; it is not legal advice.*
