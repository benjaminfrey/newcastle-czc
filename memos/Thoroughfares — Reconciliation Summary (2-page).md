## 1. What this is

Two efforts are independently writing thoroughfare standards for the same amendment cycle: **our form-based Article 3 ("Thoroughfares")** — Types **S1–S5** (Streets) and **R1–R5** (Roads), calibrated to the zoning **Districts**, with a binding classification of every existing segment — and the **Fall-2026 redline's "Article X,"** a conventional **engineering** road code (13 function-named types in two design tables) plus new definitions. Both **replace the repealed RDEO**, and both copy its culvert/ditch language nearly verbatim — so their engineering cores already overlap.

> **Bottom line.** Our form-based article is the better structural **fit** for a form-based code and is, in most respects, a **superset**; Article X contributes a short list of engineering specifics worth harvesting. The move is **merge, not choose** — and the two **cannot both reach Town Meeting**, because they define the same core terms in contradictory ways.

## 2. At a glance

| | **Our Article 3** | **Their "Article X"** |
|---|---|---|
| Lineage | SmartCode / form-based (transect) | Subdivision-road engineering (RDEO modernized) |
| Organizing axis | **Zoning District** (place) | **Function & size** (and use restriction) |
| Types | 10, place-named (Main/Village/Neighborhood/Lane/Alley; Connector/Rural/Rural-Lane/Hwy-Commercial/Rural-Hwy) | 13, function-named (Track/Small/Advisory/Local/Rural/Industrial Rd; Advisory/Local/One-Way/Connector/Industrial St + Alley) |
| Applies to | **All** thoroughfares — existing (binding map) **and** new | **New / reconstructed** only |
| Integration | Wired into Districts, frontages, setbacks, Comp Plan | Stand-alone; placeholder number; driveways punted out |
| Maturity | Complete, rendered, 215 segments classified | Draft; blank table cells; "Main St" deleted |

## 3. The core problem — conflicting definitions

Both start from the **same original CZC terms** and diverge incompatibly. Adopting both would put two contradictory definitions of the same words in one code:

| Term | **Ours** | **Theirs** |
|---|---|---|
| **Thoroughfare** | umbrella = Street + Road + **Driveway** | "a type of **right-of-way**" (excludes driveways) |
| **Street** | the **S1–S5** family (urban) | "a category for **in-town** settings" |
| **Road** | *(still the obsolete physical def)* | "a category for **rural** settings" |
| **Driveway** | access from a road to a lot | "…**not within a right-of-way**" |
| **Right-of-Way** | "total width of land reserved…" | "a **type of easement**" + Public/Private — *cleaner* |

Plus **name collisions:** both use "Connector" (ours = rural *Road*; theirs = urban *Street*), "Alley," and "Main St."

## 4. Match · differ · unique · missing

- **Genuinely match:** Street = urban / Road = rural; **typed** systems with per-type tables; **narrow calming lanes** (8–12 ft); an **Alley** at ~20 ft and the historic **rod** ROW; **engineer-stamped plans + MaineDOT specs**; **near-identical culvert/ditch** language (shared RDEO source); both replace the RDEO and use "Thoroughfare."
- **Fundamentally differ:** form/transect vs. engineering/function · District-place vs. size/use-class · all thoroughfares incl. existing (via a map) vs. new construction only · wider public-realm ROW vs. leaner utilitarian ROW · use governed by **District** vs. by **Type**.
- **Only ours:** the §5 **binding classification + GIS Type Map**; District calibration; **Ownership Categories**; **R4/R5 + §12 MaineDOT** (the Route 1 corridor); cross-section **illustrations**; design speed / grade / block length; **driveways**; Comp-Plan + frontage integration.
- **Only theirs (worth harvesting):** per-type **use/volume/vehicle** caps; **paving material & underdrainage by type**; dead-end **hammerhead** turnaround; **road-siting spacing**; one-way / advisory geometries; the **ROW-as-easement** definition.

## 5. Fit assessment & recommendation

**Ours is the better fit — clearly — because the CZC is itself a form-based code.** Every other part of it is District/transect-driven (District pages, building types, frontages, setbacks, shopfronts, use-by-District tables); our article speaks that language and plugs straight in. Article X, however competent as an engineering standard, is **structurally foreign** to a form-based code — organized by function and use-restriction, with no transect, no map, and no link to frontages or setbacks.

**Recommendation — merge:** keep our Article 3 as the framework; **harvest** Article X's engineering specifics (turnarounds, road spacing, paving/underdrainage, the precise culvert placement) into our §6/§10/§11; **borrow** their cleaner right-of-way definition; **fix** our own stale "Road" definition; **de-collide** the type names. The rest of their Fall-2026 package (the permitting/review overhaul, Project Size Standards, Article 2 changes) is **largely independent** and can proceed on its own track — only the thoroughfare piece collides.

> **The one hard rule.** Do not let both reach Town Meeting. Two contradictory definition sets, two Type systems, and two Article-3 numberings would make the adopted code internally inconsistent and litigation-prone. They must be merged into one article **before** warrant.
